Geography and History

Hills explain much of why some towns cycle and others don't, but not all of it. Towns across the North with Danish place names around them cycle more than their hills predict, the Fens of Holland more still, and towns among the names left by Norse settlers who came via Ireland cycle less. The names are a trail: they mark where people settled a thousand years ago, and the land they chose is the land where cycling works best today.

The industrial revolution: towns that stayed towns

The other half of the story. In the East, the old river, port and market towns mostly stayed separate towns, where work was a short, level ride away. In the West, the industrial revolution joined many of them into the Manchester and Liverpool conurbations, with railways, trams and long commutes. Here every flat and gently sloping town is placed by how many people travel to work in the other towns within 10 km of it, from a town on its own to one inside a conurbation, and the bars show how the typical town in each band cycles against what its hills predict.
