The Danelaw Way

A direct route along the rivers of the Danelaw, the part of England under Danish rule from the late 9th century: from the Great Army's camp at Repton and the Viking cemetery at Heath Wood, through Derby and down the Trent past the Great Army's winter camp at Torksey and Sweyn Forkbeard's base at Gainsborough, across to Howden, then up the Ouse to Viking York and on to the battlefield of 1066 at Stamford Bridge. The Lindsey Ridings loop from Torksey brings in Lincoln, Grimsby, the Humber and Scunthorpe (by the Fossdyke, the edge of the Wolds and Trent Falls) and rejoins the Way at Gainsborough, variants bring in Leicester down the Soar and Leeds down the Aire, the Boroughs of Leicester and Stamford plus Holland route runs by the Welland to the Wash, the Lindsey South and North Riding Coastal route carries on by Skegness and the Lincolnshire coast to the Humber, keeping off the Wolds, and a Skegness route leaves the Trent at Newark for Lincoln, Woodhall Spa and the sea, all on low ground. It follows the rivers rather than wandering between villages, and shows where a proper traffic-free path has to be built so nobody rides beside a busy road. England's answer to Denmark's DGI Hærvejsløbet, and far flatter.

Paths to build

Where the route has to use a road with traffic: A-road stretches of 300 m or more and B or C road stretches of 1 km or more, longest first, with the police-reported cycle collisions on each (2021–25). These are where a proper traffic-free path should be built, so nobody has to ride beside busy traffic.

Routes are proposals, routed with BRouter's touring profile through hand-picked villages on one bank of each river, so they follow the river without crossing back and forth or wandering off along signed routes between villages. They need riding and checking on the ground, especially farm tracks and towpaths after wet weather.
Riding conditions are measured every 25 m against OpenStreetMap: traffic-free is within 12 m of a cycleway or a path designated for bikes; quiet road a residential, unclassified or service road; track or lane no named road or cycle path within 12 m (unnamed lanes, farm tracks, towpaths); B or C road a secondary or tertiary road; A road a primary or trunk road. Collisions are police-reported injury collisions involving a bicycle within 30 m of the route (DfT STATS19, 2021–25). Climbing comes from BRouter's elevation data.
History is summarised from standard accounts of the Viking Age in England (the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle and recent archaeology at Repton, Heath Wood and Torksey). Place-name meanings are traditional derivations. Method and sources on GitHub.
