Long Eaton: Safe Routes to Work and School

The aim is not more cycling for its own sake. It is to get Long Eaton's workers to their jobs, and its children to school, safely and cheaply. That matters most in the town's most deprived streets. The jobs closest to them are on the business parks north of Castle Donington, and in practice only a car can reach them. A second corridor runs east along Nottingham Road to Toton, Stapleford, Beeston and Nottingham, where thousands already travel each day.

Nottingham Road: a cycle corridor in waiting

The A6005 runs east from Long Eaton's Market Place along Nottingham Road, past Toton and Chilwell, into Beeston and on to Nottingham. Toton Lane branches north to Stapleford. People already travel this way every day, in both directions.

Bridge Street and Leopold Street: options without CYCLOPS

A · Filter one side road

What: bollards or planters close Leopold Street (or Bridge Street) to motor traffic at Derby Road; bikes and people on foot still get through.

For: removes one junction's turning conflicts outright; cheap and quick; a trial can use temporary planters.

Against: traffic re-routes to the other side road; access for residents and deliveries must be checked.

B · Ban the right turns

What: left in, left out only at both side roads, with a central island on Derby Road.

For: removes the right-turn crashes, the most common type here; keeps both streets open to traffic.

Against: leaves left hooks; drivers may U-turn elsewhere; the island needs carriageway width.

C · Raised continuous crossings at both

What: carry the pavement and a protected track across both side-road mouths at their own level, with tightened corners, and a raised table on Derby Road between them.

For: slows every turning driver; drivers give way to cyclists and people walking, as the Highway Code says; no traffic is banned.

Against: relies on drivers yielding (about 70% did on trial side-road zebras); needs room for the track.

These can be combined: A at Leopold Street with C at Bridge Street is the most protective. Which works best depends on traffic counts and street widths not checked here. If Derby Road is too narrow for protected tracks, a parallel quiet route through the side streets, with filtered crossings, is the fallback.
