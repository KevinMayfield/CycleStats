# Long Eaton: safe routes to work and school

*A case study and proposal, written 30 October 2026, drawing on the CycleStats analysis and the Long Eaton–Castle
Donington cycle link study. The web version, with a map and diagrams:
<https://kevinmayfield.github.io/CycleStats/longeaton.html>*

**The aim is not more cycling for its own sake.** It is to get Long Eaton's workers to their jobs, and its children to
school, safely and cheaply. That matters most in the town's most deprived streets. The jobs closest to them, on the
business parks north of Castle Donington, can in practice only be reached by car. A second corridor runs east along
Nottingham Road to Toton, Stapleford, Beeston and Nottingham, where thousands already travel each day.

## 1. Why Long Eaton

**Long Eaton is built for cycling, and has the habit already.** It sits on the flat floor of the Trent valley. Most
trips are short: 40% of commuters travel under 5 km and another 27% between 5 and 10 km.

- **It does better than its circumstances predict.** Our model of every town compares hills, history, commute
  distances, car ownership, deprivation, rain and region. Long Eaton cycles **about a quarter more** than the model
  predicts (4.3% against 3.5%), so something local is still working.
- **The habit is long-standing.** In 2011:
  - 7.3% of working residents in the six most deprived areas cycled to work, more than twice the England rate.
  - 14% of pupils at The Long Eaton School cycled to school, higher than 96% of English secondaries.
  - At Wilsthorpe it was 9%, higher than 92% of them.

**But it is fading.** Cycling to work fell from 6.0% in 2011 to 4.3% in 2021.

- **The habit is ageing.** People aged 50–64 are 1.27 times as likely to cycle to work as those aged 16–34. That is
  the pattern we found in towns where cycling is ageing out: the people who learned it still ride, but fewer young
  people take it up.
- **Few women ride.** Women are 25% of cycle commuters. That is fewer than predicted for a flat town, and about 20%
  below our model's estimate for women here.
- **Long Eaton is risky for each cyclist.** Collisions per cyclist are in the highest quarter of towns, at an index
  of 161 where a typical town is 100. 31% of its cycle collisions are serious.

**Its industrial past only partly explains this.** Long Eaton sits on the edge of the coalfield, not in it: the
nearest colliery was 6.8 km from its built-up edge. Its trades were lace, furniture, the railway and iron. Two big
employers were a short ride from home:

- **Toton Sidings**, 1.5 km away, was Europe's largest marshalling yard at the height of the coal traffic. It was in
  decline by the 1970s: the north yard closed in 1978 and the up side in 1984, and it was cut back to its present
  shape in the late 1980s.
- **Stanton Ironworks**, 5 km north, opened in 1846 by the Nutbrook Canal. It employed about 7,000 at its height
  under British Steel, and was later run by Stanton and Staveley and then Saint-Gobain. The last pipe was cast on
  24 May 2007, and the 470-acre site is now proposed for housing.

The work moved to sites by the motorway junctions and into the cities, planned around the car. Today 1,179 Long Eaton
residents work north of Castle Donington, by M1 junction 24, the A50, the airport and East Midlands Gateway.
1,819 work in Nottingham and 1,354 in Derby. Seventy per cent of commuters drive. Trips that once went on foot, by
bike or by train now funnel by car through the same pinch points: M1 junctions 24, 24A and 25, Harrington Bridge and
the A6005. That is a big part of the area's peak-hour congestion, though no traffic counts are included here.

**The case to act now:** the town still has the habit, the terrain and the short distances. Without safe routes, the
habit is ageing out instead of passing to women, younger people and children.

## 2. How people get to work today

Census 2021, residents of Long Eaton travelling to work:

| How they travel | Share of commuters |
|---|---|
| Driving a car or van | **69.5%** |
| On foot | 10.5% |
| Bus | **6.2%** |
| Passenger in a car | 5.5% |
| Bicycle | **4.3%** |
| Train, tram, motorcycle, taxi, other | 4.0% |

A further 26% of people in work worked mainly from home. The Census was taken in March 2021, during a lockdown, so
that figure was high.

**Where they work** (people who travel to a workplace, Census 2021):

| Destination | Workers | Share | Distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Within Long Eaton | 3,533 | 28% | under 2 km |
| Nottingham | 1,819 | 14% | 9–11 km |
| Broxtowe (Beeston, Toton, Stapleford) | 1,394 | 11% | 2–6 km |
| Derby | 1,354 | 11% | 8–15 km |
| Erewash outside Long Eaton | 1,200 | 10% | 3–9 km |
| **North of Castle Donington** (East Midlands Airport, Willow Farm and Trent Lane, the logistics parks) | **1,179** | **9%** | **7–8 km** |

**The car paradox.** Castle Donington is a dormitory village with few workers of its own. The jobs on its northern
edge are taken by people who drive in.

- **Who travels there:** 10.4% of Long Eaton workers from households without a car work there, the highest share of
  any group. For two-car households it is 5.7%.
- **What they earn:** 8.3% of the town's lowest-paid workers (semi-skilled and unskilled) go there, against 1.6% of
  managers and professionals.

These are the people least able to afford a car, travelling to the jobs that are hardest to reach without one.

### The Nottingham Road corridor

The A6005 runs east from the Market Place along Nottingham Road, past Toton and Chilwell, into Beeston and on to
Nottingham. Toton Lane branches north to Stapleford.

| Between Long Eaton and | Out of Long Eaton | Into Long Eaton | Distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toton and Chilwell West | 283 | 321 | 2–3 km |
| Stapleford | 207 | 321 | 4–5 km |
| Beeston and Chilwell | 774 | 301 | 4–6 km |
| Nottingham | 1,794 | 547 | 9–11 km |
| **Total** | **3,058** | **1,490** | **4,548 a day** |

- **People already cycle it.** The Propensity to Cycle Tool puts 103–259 cycle commuters a day on the A6005 between
  Long Eaton and Beeston in 2011.
- **At Dutch levels** it would be 584–1,083, or up to 1,413 with e-bikes.
- **Secondary schools along the way** add children's trips: Chilwell School, George Spencer Academy, Alderman White
  School, Bramcote College and Trent College.

A protected Nottingham Road (path 1) is the first link of that corridor. It also joins the Danelaw Cycle Network's
Derby–Nottingham route.

## 3. Bus, car and bike: door-to-door times to the Castle Donington business parks

These come from the corridor study. Each trip starts at the population-weighted centre of one of Long Eaton's six
most deprived small areas, and ends at the Willow Farm and Trent Lane business parks.

How each time was worked out:
- **Car:** routing plus 3 minutes to park.
- **Bus:** the my15 timetable, with the walk to and from the stops and 5 minutes' wait.
- **Bike on new paths:** an assumed 18 km/h, or 22 km/h on an e-bike.

| Start (IMD 2025 decile) | Car | my15 bus | Bike today | Bike, new paths | E-bike, new paths |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A · Coniston Road (1, most deprived) | 15 | 50 | 37 | **30** | **25** |
| B · Bank Street (2) | 14 | 36 | 33 | 26 | 22 |
| C · Beresford Road, Sawley (2) | 11 | 23 | 23 | 19 | 16 |
| D · Hamilton Road (2) | 15 | 37 | 33 | 26 | 22 |
| E · Ash Grove (3) | 12 | 35 | 27 | 22 | 18 |
| F · Oakley's Road (3) | 14 | 36 | 30 | 25 | 21 |

- **The bus is slowest from the poorest streets.** The my15 is the only direct service. It runs every 30 minutes in
  the daytime but hourly before 06:00 and after 19:00, exactly when shifts start and finish. From Coniston Road it
  takes 50 minutes, more than three times as long as the car.
- **The new paths bring cycling close to the car.** With them, a pedal bike takes 19–30 minutes and an e-bike 16–25
  minutes, against 11–15 by car. From most areas that is within 10 minutes of driving, and quicker than the bus
  everywhere.
- **Time is not the only barrier.** Today's route runs along 30–40 mph roads with no protection, over Harrington
  Bridge on a 40 mph carriageway, and through the A50 roundabouts, often in the dark at shift changes. Few people will
  ride that at any speed. What the paths change most is whether people ride at all.

## 4. Children and school

The two secondary schools both sit on the proposed network:
- **The Long Eaton School** is just off Tamworth Road.
- **Wilsthorpe School** is on Derby Road at Wilsthorpe Island.

Nearly all their pupils (94% and 97%) live within 5 km, on routes averaging 0.3–0.4% gradient. A typical English
secondary's routes average 1.6%.

| Secondary schools, 2011 school census | Pupils cycling |
|---|---|
| All English secondaries (2,654) | 3.8% |
| Flat, compact English secondaries (343, a like-for-like comparison) | 7.0% |
| Derbyshire and Derby | 2.3% |
| **The Long Eaton School** | **13.9%** |
| **Wilsthorpe School** | **9.2%** |
| Cambridge, England's highest | 48.7% |
| The two schools at Dutch rates (Go Dutch) | 77–79% |

**Three more secondaries sit on the Nottingham Road corridor** (Propensity to Cycle Tool, 2011 school census):

| School | Town | Pupils | Route gradient within 5 km | Cycling, 2011 | Go Dutch | Driven, 2011 → Go Dutch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Long Eaton School | Long Eaton | 1,287 | 0.3% | 13.9% (179) | 79% | 53 → 11 |
| Wilsthorpe School | Long Eaton | 824 | 0.4% | 9.2% (76) | 77% | 80 → 15 |
| Chilwell School | Chilwell | 1,059 | 0.8% | **14.2% (150)** | 83% | 83 → 18 |
| George Spencer Academy | Stapleford and Toton | 1,359 | 1.9% | 5.4% (73) | 70% | 170 → 55 |
| Friesland School | Sandiacre | 1,351 | 2.7% | **0.9% (12)** | 64% | 324 → 103 |
| **All five** | | **5,880** | | **8.3% (490)** | **74% (4,341)** | **710 → 202** |

- **Where the routes are flat, children cycle.** Chilwell School and The Long Eaton School sit on the flat Trent
  valley floor and were in England's top tenth.
- **The hillier schools cycle less.** George Spencer and Friesland draw from the slopes up towards Stapleford,
  Bramcote and Stanton.
- **Friesland stands out.** It also has the fewest pupils within 5 km (76%) and the most driven to school (324).
- **Even the hillier schools could reach high levels.** Go Dutch puts George Spencer at 70% and Friesland at 64%.
  Across the five schools that is 4,341 pupils cycling instead of 490, and about 500 fewer driven.

Even against other flat, compact schools, Long Eaton's pupils cycled about twice as much, with no protected network.
Nationally, cycling to school has since fallen to about 2% of trips by 11–16-year-olds (National Travel Survey 2024).
No school-level figures have been collected since 2011.

**Children are being hurt.** Of the 79 cyclists injured in Long Eaton in 2021–25, **11 were under 16**, and 6 of
those were hurt at school-run times. Wilsthorpe Island, the roundabout at Wilsthorpe School's gate, is one of the
town's collision hotspots.

## 5. Where people are hurt

Police-recorded cycle collisions in Long Eaton, 2021–25: **78**, of which **24 killed or seriously injured**.

| What happened | Long Eaton | The five towns with the most cycle collisions |
|---|---|---|
| Driver turning right across the cyclist | **19%** | 12–19% |
| **At a roundabout** | **18%** | 2–10% |
| Driver turning left across the cyclist | **14%** | 7–17% |
| Both going ahead, paths crossing at a junction | 13% | 13–17% |
| Driver overtaking or changing lane | 9% | 3–4% |
| Other | 27% | |

- **Most crashes are at junctions.** Over half (53%) are at T-junctions and 18% at roundabouts. Counting crossroads
  and other junctions, more than 80% are at a junction of some kind.
- **Turning traffic and roundabouts are the problem.** Drivers turning across the cyclist, plus roundabouts, account
  for half of all collisions. Long Eaton's roundabout share is almost twice that of any of the five.
- **Speed limits don't explain it.** 87% of collisions are on 30 mph roads.

**Hotspots** (three or more cycle collisions within 75 m, 2021–25):

| Location | Collisions | Serious | What happens there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Derby Road (A6005) / Market Place roundabout | 6 | 3 | Roundabout collisions |
| Derby Road (A6005) at Bridge Street and Leopold Street | 5 | 1 | Drivers turning left and right across cyclists |
| Wilsthorpe Island (Derby Road / Wilsthorpe Road), by Wilsthorpe School | 3 | 2 | Roundabout collisions |
| Tamworth Road (B6540) at Bradshaw Street | 3 | 1 | Parked cars, overtaking, hit from behind |
| Tamworth Road (B6540) at Wyvern Avenue | 3 | 1 | Side-road turns and pulling out |

- **Protected junctions, Dutch-style roundabouts, tighter corners and 20 mph past the schools** are road-safety
  schemes as much as cycle schemes.

## 6. The proposal

### Routes

Seven paths, from the corridor study:

1. **Nottingham Road (A6005), 1.2 km:** kerb-separated tracks from the east side into the Market Place.
2. **Derby Road (A6005), 2.3 km:** protected tracks from the Breaston edge past Wilsthorpe School to the Market Place.
3. **Wilsthorpe Road (B6002), 1.8 km:** protected tracks north–south through the west of town to Tamworth Road at
   the station.
4. **Tamworth Road (B6540), 3.1 km:** a protected track from the Market Place to Sawley. This is the spine every
   work trip uses, and the route to The Long Eaton School.
5. **Harrington Bridge, 0.4 km:** a separate path over the Trent and Sawley Cut, away from the 40 mph carriageway.
6. **B6540 to Castle Donington, 2.1 km:** a parallel off-road path, lit for shift times.
7. **A50 junction and Station Road, 1.7 km:** into the business parks.

Together they make each trip to the business parks about a fifth quicker. They connect both secondary schools, the
town centre and the six most deprived areas to the jobs north of Castle Donington. They also join the Danelaw Cycle
Network's Derby–Nottingham route.

### Junctions

Most of the danger is at junctions. Painted lanes would not help: on these areas' roads they are no safer than
nothing.

| Junction | Treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Derby Road / Market Place roundabout | **Dutch-style roundabout**: a cycle track round the outside, with drivers giving way to cyclists and pedestrians on each arm | The town's worst hotspot: 6 collisions, 3 serious |
| Wilsthorpe Island (Derby Road / Wilsthorpe Road) | **Dutch-style roundabout** | 3 collisions, 2 serious, at Wilsthorpe School's gate |
| Derby Road at Bridge Street and Leopold Street | **No room for CYCLOPS.** Options, which can be combined: (A) filter Leopold Street to motor traffic at Derby Road; (B) left in, left out only at both side roads; (C) raised continuous crossings at both, with tightened corners | 5 collisions, from turning traffic |
| Tamworth Road / Wilsthorpe Road at the station, and Sawley | **CYCLOPS** where signal-controlled; otherwise a Dutch-style roundabout | Where paths 3 and 4 meet; every Sawley trip passes through |
| Side roads along Tamworth Road, Derby Road and Nottingham Road | **Continuous footway and cycle track** across each side road, so drivers turning in give way | Turning and pulling-out collisions at Wyvern Avenue, Bradshaw Street and others |
| A50 junction and Station Road roundabouts, Castle Donington | **Dutch-style roundabouts**, or signal crossings or underpasses where traffic is too heavy | Without them, workers must ride through motorway-junction roundabouts |

Dutch-style roundabouts are still rare in the UK. Cambridge's Fendon Road (2020) was the first full one, and its
early record was mixed while drivers learned to give way. Manchester has built several CYCLOPS junctions since 2020,
but they have not yet been independently evaluated. Long Eaton could be a well-measured example of both.

### Alongside the routes

- **An e-bike loan or purchase scheme for workers on the business parks.** It brings five of the six deprived areas
  to around 20 minutes from work, and would work best agreed with the employers.
- **Lighting and maintenance for shift times.** The paths beside the B6540 and the A50 will be used in the dark.
- **School streets and bike storage** at both secondaries and their feeder primaries.
- **A simple monitoring plan:** bike counts at both schools and the business parks, before and after.

## 7. What it could change

The Propensity to Cycle Tool (PCT) is the Department for Transport-funded model councils use to plan cycle networks.
Its base year is 2011.

**Commuters in the six most deprived areas** (3,943 working residents, all workplaces):

| Scenario | Cycle to work | Drive to work | Fewer drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 Census | 7% (287) | 57% (2,238) | |
| Government Target | 12% (469) | 54% | 98 |
| Go Dutch | 28% (1,118) | 46% | **427**, about 850 fewer car trips each working day |
| Go Dutch with e-bikes | 32% (1,279) | 43% | **540**, about a quarter of car commuters |

**Pupils at the two secondaries:**

| | The Long Eaton School | Wilsthorpe School |
|---|---|---|
| Cycling, 2011 | 14% (179) | 9% (76) |
| Government Target | 20% | 15% |
| Go Dutch | 79% (1,013) | 77% (637) |
| Driven to school, 2011 → Go Dutch | 53 → 11 | 80 → 15 |

**Across the whole town**, Go Dutch would mean 26% of commuters cycling, against 4.3% in 2021.

**Safety.** On our roads, stretches with a protected cycle track have about a third fewer cycle collisions than roads
with nothing, and about half the serious ones. Long Eaton's danger is concentrated at a few junctions and along four
main roads, so protecting those would cover most of the places where people are hurt.

**Health and household budgets.** All six deprived areas are in England's most deprived fifth for health, and
6.5–9.0% of residents report bad or very bad health, against 5.2% in England. A cheap, reliable way to work that
builds in daily activity serves both problems. It also lowers the cost of a car that many households cannot afford.

## 8. What this does not show, and what to check

- **These are estimates, not forecasts.**
  - The PCT scenarios assume a complete, safe network, and use 2011 travel.
  - The new-path journey times assume a speed, not one that was measured.
  - The 2021 Census was taken in a lockdown, so part of the fall in cycling reflects home working.
- **Collisions are police-recorded,** which misses many minor ones. Recorded causes (contributory factors) are not
  public, so the crash types come from what each vehicle was doing.
- **Nothing has been checked on site.** Before designing anything, check:
  - road widths, and room on Harrington Bridge
  - land for a path beside the B6540
  - the A50 junction layout
  - which junctions are signal-controlled
- **Three highway authorities** share the route: Derbyshire County Council, Leicestershire County Council and National
  Highways. No one owns the whole journey, so the scheme needs a joint sponsor.
- **Current school figures would strengthen the case.** No school-level travel data has been published since 2011.
  Hands-up surveys or bike-rack counts at both secondaries would give a current baseline.
