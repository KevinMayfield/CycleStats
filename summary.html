Most popular mixes Leeds City Council automatic counters (12-month averages) with DfT traffic counts, which are mostly single weekday counts on main roads. Some counters record one direction of a one-way lane, and a road can appear twice where it has two count points.
Collisions are police-reported injury collisions involving a bicycle (DfT STATS19, 2021–2025), matched to the nearest named road. A road is a connected stretch of one name, so long roads collect more.
Risk per rider = collisions ÷ (bikes per day × length × 365 × 5) × 1,000,000, for roads with 5+ collisions and a DfT cycle count. Where bike counts are low, a few collisions give a high rate, so treat the order as indicative.
Deprivation uses the English Indices of Deprivation 2025: the most deprived fifth means neighbourhoods (LSOAs) in deciles 1–2 for England as a whole. Collisions are placed in the neighbourhood STATS19 records for them. Each area map has a Neighbourhoods layer showing deprivation, housing condition and households with no car.
Safe & busy lists roads with 150+ bikes a day and the fewest collisions per km cycled. Off-road paths have no police collision data, so none appear here. Method and sources on GitHub.
